An inventory doesn't have to be a list of achievements.

This sample note begins somewhere quieter: with the things that made an ordinary week a little better. A familiar walk. A conversation without a clock. A recipe that became easy enough to make without instructions.

## Keep, change, leave behind

It helps to use smaller categories than *success* and *failure*.

| Keep | Change | Leave behind |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Walking without headphones sometimes | Making room for a longer lunch | Checking every notification |
| Writing things down before forgetting | Finishing one small project at a time | Turning every hobby into a target |
| Calling someone just to catch up | Reading slowly instead of collecting links | Saving everything for the perfect day |

These aren't resolutions. They're observations that might make the next week easier.

## Not everything needs measuring

The trouble with a scorecard is that you start looking for things that fit inside it.

A good afternoon doesn't always have a result. A book can matter even if you don't finish it. A conversation can be valuable because it wandered rather than because it arrived.

Perhaps the useful question isn't, "What did I accomplish?"

Perhaps it is, "What would I like to make room for again?"
