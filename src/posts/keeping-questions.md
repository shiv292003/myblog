An answer is satisfying to write down. It has edges. You can underline it and move on.

A question is less tidy. It stays open, asking for another page.

This sample article is about giving those open questions a place to live.

## A different kind of notebook

Instead of only recording what you learned, try recording what confused you:

1. What did I expect to happen?
2. What happened instead?
3. What would help me understand the difference?

That small shift makes a notebook less like a trophy cabinet and more like a workbench.

## Return later

Some questions become easier after you've spent time doing something else. Others reveal that you were asking the wrong thing.

Either way, an old question can show you how your thinking has changed. An answer alone rarely tells that whole story.
