There is something reassuring about a website that doesn't ask much of you. A title, a few links, a page of writing. You arrive, read something, and leave when you're ready.

This is a sample essay for that kind of place.

## A place, not a platform

A personal site can be modest in a way that most of the internet no longer allows. It doesn't need a content strategy or a reason for every paragraph to exist. It can hold an unfinished thought next to an essay you spent weeks getting right.

The point isn't to reach everyone. It's to have somewhere that feels like your own.

> A small place can still hold a lot, if you give things enough room.

## Start with what matters

It is tempting to begin with features. Search, reactions, elaborate categories, a dozen ways to subscribe. But the useful question comes before all of that: what would I like to put here?

For a first version, the answer might be:

- A few pieces of writing worth keeping.
- An introduction to the person behind them.
- A way to return when something new appears.

Everything else can wait until it has a reason.

## Leave room to change

The nicest thing about a small site is that it doesn't have to be finished. You can move the furniture. Rewrite the introduction. Discover that the topic you expected to write about isn't the one you keep returning to.

Begin with a page. Let the rest grow from using it.
